# Redirected

The UK tax system wasn't designed. It accumulated. Over decades, decisions were made, exceptions were carved out, and political compromises were struck. The result is a system that taxes work at every turn while largely exempting idle wealth, land value, and pollution.

This is my attempt to work out what a tax system designed from scratch — one that rewards work and enterprise while taxing what we want less of — might actually look like. Not as a political manifesto. Not as an academic exercise. As a citizen following the logic wherever it goes.

## The argument

The economy rewards the wrong things, not because of malice but because of accumulation. The fix is redirection, not restraint. Don't ask people to want less. Don't rely on moralising. Change the price signals: tax extraction, pollution, idle capital and land banking; stop taxing work, enterprise and creation.

Good systems redirect human behaviour rather than suppress it. That is the operating principle for everything in this book.
