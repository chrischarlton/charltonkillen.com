Joel, the nurse in Chapter 6 of [the book](/redirected/book/), moves back to Warrington from London. He is £650 a month better off. Rents are lower. The commute is manageable. He can afford to think about buying. He wants to be part of it.

He is not a special case. He is the archetype. The reform does not redistribute from one group to another arbitrarily; it corrects a structural skew that has been accumulating for thirty years, one that systematically advantages those who own assets over those who earn wages. Correcting it makes the most difference to those who have had the least time to accumulate.
