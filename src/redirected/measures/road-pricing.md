evidence: |
  <p>VED is a blunt instrument. It charges the same whether you drive 1,000 miles or 30,000. Fuel duty is better — it correlates with use — but it cannot distinguish between a rural driver with no alternative and an urban commuter choosing to drive at peak hour when the road is already congested.</p>
  <p>Road pricing charges for actual use of a shared resource, with the price reflecting the actual cost imposed: congestion at peak times, wear on urban roads, emissions from the journey. Stockholm's congestion pricing reduced traffic by 22% and cut emissions by 14% — and was rejected in a referendum, then re-introduced by the same population that rejected it, once the effects were visible. The design lesson: make the benefits as visible as the cost.</p>

> Replace Vehicle Excise Duty (commonly known as ‘road tax’) and a portion of fuel duty with dynamic **Road Pricing**.

Road use is tracked via an independent GPS system which strict privacy regulations, public oversight and accountability. Only the data needed to charge accurately is gathered.

<table class="rd-fiscal-table">
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Vehicle type</th><th>Base rate per mile</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Electric vehicle</td><td>2p</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Petrol or diesel car</td><td>7p</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Van or truck</td><td>15p</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

Those base rates are then adjusted by location and time:

<table class="rd-fiscal-table">
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Condition</th><th>Multiplier</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>City centre, peak hours</td><td>×2.5</td></tr>
    <tr><td>City centre, off-peak</td><td>×1.5</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Motorway</td><td>×1.0</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Rural roads</td><td>×0.7</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Night (11pm–6am)</td><td>×0.5</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

What this means in practice for three different drivers — all of whom currently pay VED (around £200/year) and fuel duty on top:

<table class="rd-fiscal-table">
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Driver</th><th>Under road pricing</th><th>Currently (VED + fuel duty)</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Rural driver, 5,000 miles, mostly off-peak</td><td>£275/year</td><td>£550/year</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Suburban commuter, 10,000 miles, 30% peak</td><td>£625/year</td><td>£900/year</td></tr>
    <tr><td>City commuter, 12,000 miles, 50% peak</td><td>£900/year</td><td>£1,040/year</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

Once you pay annual road tax (VED) it makes sense to drive as much as possible to spread the cost. When you only pay per mile and when the type of miles you drive are charged at different rates, you are empowered to make different choices.
